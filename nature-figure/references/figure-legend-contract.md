# Figure Legend Contract

Read this reference when writing, revising, or auditing a figure or table
legend. Verify the target journal's current instructions before final
submission; Nature-family patterns are defaults, not universal rules.

## Structure

For a multi-panel research figure, use:

1. `Fig. N |` followed by a short noun phrase or evidence-calibrated finding
   that names the figure as a whole;
2. panel descriptions in `a`, `b`, `c` order;
3. the information needed to interpret the visual without searching the body;
4. statistical and provenance details required by the journal.

Do not force a finding into the title line when the evidence is descriptive or
the target journal prefers nominal titles.

## Panel Language

- Describe visible content in the present tense.
- Describe how an analysis, sample, or image was produced in the past tense.
- State color, shape, line, symbol, and spatial-region mappings when they are
  not obvious from direct labels.
- Name units and define abbreviations at first use.
- Keep each panel description tied to what the panel actually shows.

## Statistical Minimum

For each quantitative panel, include or point unambiguously to:

- what `n` counts;
- biological, technical, spatial, temporal, or repeated units as relevant;
- center and spread or interval;
- statistical test and multiple-comparison treatment when applicable;
- exact comparison or model contrast;
- source-data location when required.

Do not paste a generic statistical checklist into the legend when a field or
journal uses a different reporting convention.

## Self-Containment And Claim Strength

A reader should understand the visual encoding, sample basis, and comparison
without reconstructing them from Methods. A final interpretive sentence is
optional. Include it only when the panels directly support the inference; do
not convert an association or prediction figure into a mechanism claim.

## Maps And Spatial Figures

State the mapped quantity, unit, time period, spatial unit or resolution, and
meaning of classification or uncertainty. Include projection or scale details
when they affect measurement or interpretation, not as ceremonial metadata.

## Adapted Material

Identify adapted or reproduced panels and preserve the exact attribution and
permission language required by the source license and target journal.

## Source Data Wording

Use a journal-specific Source Data statement only when a Source Data file
exists and the journal requests that wording. Never append boilerplate that
promises a file that is absent.

## Final Check

- Figure title does not overpromise.
- Panel order matches the visual.
- Legend defines encodings, units, and abbreviations.
- Statistics match the plotted data and analysis.
- Attribution is complete.
- No private local path or internal template name is disclosed.

## Caption To Manuscript Alignment

Use the evidence trace in `figure-contract.md`. Match each caption assertion to
source data and its transformation, and each manuscript claim citing the figure
to what the figure actually supports. Split compound causal/mechanistic and
quantitative claims for this check. A correct image and matching numbers are
necessary but do not establish the interpretation. Preserve necessary scope and
uncertainty; highlight a supported advantage without hiding material evidence.
