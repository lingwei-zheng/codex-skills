# SSCI Section Playbook

Use this reference for social-science journal manuscripts, SSCI-targeted papers,
or requests for section-level writing guidance. It supplements the selected
paper structure; it does not override the target journal, the user's explicit
requirements, or the study design.

## Priority

Apply requirements in this order:

1. Target-journal author guidelines.
2. The user's explicit structure request.
3. Paper type and research design.
4. This playbook.

Treat `起承转合` as a rhetorical check:

- `起`: establish the problem and reader relevance.
- `承`: develop the evidence or scholarly context.
- `转`: identify the unresolved tension and the paper's response.
- `合`: state what the evidence permits the reader to conclude.

Do not force these moves into four paragraphs or use them to manufacture drama.

## Standalone Literature Review Gate

Do not create a standalone `Literature Review` section by default.

Include one only when:

- the user explicitly requests `Literature Review`, `文献综述章节`, or an
  equivalent standalone section; or
- verified target-journal instructions require it.

If journal instructions require the section but the user did not request it,
state that requirement before adding it. Generic requests such as "write a
paper", "create an outline", "use IMRaD", "target an SSCI journal", or "review
the literature" do not by themselves authorize a standalone section.

Without a standalone section:

- synthesize only the literature needed to establish context, concepts,
  debates, and the research gap in the Introduction;
- use the Discussion for comparison with prior findings, theoretical
  interpretation, competing explanations, and implications;
- do not duplicate the same literature summary in both locations.

When a standalone section is authorized, organize it by theory, theme, debate,
mechanism, or methodological tradition. Use an
independent-variable/dependent-variable/relationship structure only for a
genuinely variable-centred quantitative study where that organization clarifies
the argument.

## Section Moves

### Abstract

Use `references/abstract_writing_guide.md` as the authority. The normal flow is:

`context/problem -> purpose -> method -> key findings -> bounded implication`

Do not repeat a miniature literature review or promise results that are not
reported.

### Introduction

Move from broad relevance to the paper's precise contribution:

1. Establish the phenomenon, problem, and stakes for the target audience.
2. Define the necessary concepts and synthesize the most relevant scholarship.
3. Identify a specific gap, contradiction, unresolved mechanism, or limitation
   that changes what can currently be known or done.
4. State the research question, objective, or hypothesis.
5. Explain the study's approach and contribution at the strength supported by
   the evidence.

General journals may need more background than specialist journals. Do not
announce a generic "little is known" gap without showing what remains unresolved
and why it matters.

### Methods

Organize Methods for reproducibility and evaluation, not by a mandatory
chronological narrative. Use the sequence appropriate to the design, such as:

`design -> setting/sample/data -> measures/materials -> procedure -> analysis
-> validity/robustness -> ethics`

Include only components that apply to the study.

### Results

Organize Results by research question, claim, analytic task, or evidence chain.
Do not mechanically mirror Methods.

- Open each subsection or paragraph with the finding it establishes.
- Use tables and figures as evidence anchors, not as the outline itself.
- Report conditions, estimates, uncertainty, and robustness needed to evaluate
  the finding.
- Reserve interpretation and literature comparison for Discussion unless the
  journal convention explicitly combines Results and Discussion.

### Discussion

Use this sequence as appropriate:

1. State the central finding and answer the research question.
2. Compare the finding with relevant prior work.
3. Explain the theoretical, methodological, practical, or policy significance.
4. Evaluate competing explanations and evidence boundaries.
5. Identify useful future research that follows from unresolved issues.

Do not claim that the paper "significantly advances the field" unless the
evidence and comparison support that judgment. Include limitations when they
change interpretation, validity, identification, generalisability, or scope;
avoid generic self-diminishing disclaimers.

### Conclusion

Close in one or two compact paragraphs:

1. Restate the central contribution without copying the Introduction.
2. Compress the decisive evidence or result.
3. State what changes for theory, method, practice, or policy.
4. Add a boundary or future direction only when it improves correct
   interpretation.

Do not introduce new evidence, citations, or emotional appeals.

## Submission Package

In submission mode, verify the target journal before producing:

- title page;
- highlights;
- abstract and keywords;
- main manuscript;
- tables;
- figure captions;
- figures;
- cover letter;
- declarations or supplementary files.

Generate only required or user-requested components. Preserve the manuscript's
approved section structure during formatting; formatting must not add a
standalone Literature Review.
